Every source vs the result

civicAPI publishes its own forecast and republishes eighteen more, from outlets, models and markets. As each race is called, this page counts how many of them had it right, how many hedged with a Tossup, and how many had it wrong.

How it is scored. Every source is read through its nine-step rating, the one thing they all publish: Safe, Likely, Lean or Tilt toward a party, or Tossup. A rating that leans toward a party is a call, and it is right if that party won and wrong if the other one did (an independent winner makes every call wrong). A Tossup is no call: it is the so-so column, neither a hit nor a miss. The bar splits each column by how firm the rating was: Safe and Likely are firm, Lean and Tilt are narrow, so a wrong firm call is the worst result and a right narrow call the least impressive. Right of calls is right divided by right plus wrong.

The Brier score is for sources that publish odds: for each called race it takes the squared gap between their chance of a Democratic win and what happened (1 or 0), times two so that a coin flip scores 0.5. Zero is perfect, lower is better. Races an independent won are left out. Sources cover different races, so compare Brier scores loosely.

What was saved. The forecasts are the copies civicAPI served on the morning of 4 November, dated 3 November, frozen by us before the count had moved them; they are not live prices. Four sources are left out because they are too thin to compare: PredictIt covers only part of the House and the governorships, and Decision Desk HQ, RRH Elections and OpenSourceZone were last updated weeks or months ago. civicAPI's own "experimental" feed is empty and is not shown.
